The Brenner line runs from Innsbruck to Verona through the heart of the Alps, and it stops at Brixen, a small cathedral town where the valleys of the western Dolomites begin. From the station, regional buses climb into each of them on a timetable you can set your watch by.

You do not need a car here. Many South Tyrol hotels give guests a pass for regional buses and trains, and in winter the ski buses run the same routes.

## Arrive by train

From Munich, the direct train takes about four hours. From Verona, about two. Ask for a window seat: the river, the castles and the first pale peaks pass close to the line.

## Walk from the door

Choose a house where the trail starts at the gate. In Val di Funes, the paths to the alms under the Odle peaks begin in the village and climb through larch forest to meadows where the huts serve lunch until the cows come home.

> You do not need a car here.

## Ride when your legs say so

The bus network is the secret. Walk one way, ride back; walk over a pass into the next valley and let the bus return you for dinner. Timetables are posted at every stop and the drivers will tell you if the last one is about to leave.

## Pack light

A daypack, rain shell, layers and real boots. Everything else can stay at the house. The hut will have soup, the village will have a shop, and the view is free.
