North of the Arctic Circle in January, the sun barely clears the mountains. People expect darkness. What they find instead is light that never quite decides what time it is: long pink dawns that run straight into long blue dusks, with a short bright day in between.

## The blue hour lasts all afternoon

Photographers come for it, but you do not need a camera. The snow on the peaks turns rose, then violet, then a blue so deep it looks painted. The red houses on the shore glow against it like lanterns.

## The aurora keeps its own timetable

Clear, cold nights are best, and they cannot be booked. Stay at least four nights to give the sky a fair chance. Most houses will wake you if it appears; say yes when they ask.

> Light that never quite decides what time it is.

## Days are for the sea

Short days still have room for a lot. Take a boat out among the islands, walk the beaches on the outer coast, eat dried cod in a village that has been drying cod for a thousand years.

## Then come inside

Winter here is built for evenings. Sauna, a long dinner, a window that faces the water, and the chance that the sky might start moving at any minute.
